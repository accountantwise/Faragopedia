Why not just buy a CRM?

A CRM is a system of record — a filing cabinet someone has to fill in by hand. What we built is a system of intelligence: it finds the leads, verifies the contacts, writes the record, and turns every meeting into knowledge that compounds. The real choice isn't “CRM vs. this.” It's a database someone must feed vs. a loop that feeds itself.

The step a CRM can't do: accumulated intelligence about who we know, why they mattered, and what worked feeds straight back into next week's targeting. The system sharpens every cycle — on its own.

Three things a subscription CRM won't do
01

It finds who to approach, not just their email

CRMs can enrich a contact from a name & company — but only as a paid add-on, once you've decided who that is. Ours does the deciding: fit-calibrated targets each week, verified and written in.

Per contact: ~30 min of hunting → 3–5 sec
02

Knowledge compounds instead of sitting in fields

A CRM stores flat records. Faragopedia builds a self-maintaining knowledge graph — every meeting enriches an interlinked web of people, companies & context.

Output = institutional memory, not a list
03

It lives where the work already happens

No new tool, no migration, no seats to train. Prospects surface in the Google Sheet in use; intelligence surfaces in a wiki anyone can read.

Adoption cost: zero new software

What the off-the-shelf route actually costs

  • CRM seats — $12–$175+ per user / month
  • A data-enrichment add-on to auto-fill contacts — ~$49–$99/mo or ~$0.20+/contact overage
  • Integration middleware (Zapier / Make) to wire it together
  • Onboarding $1,500–$3,500; certified consultants $100–$300/hr
  • … and you'd still prospect by hand

Typical 2026 market pricing. The sticker price is only the first line — and none of it decides who to approach for you.

Where a CRM genuinely wins

  • Mature deal-pipeline mechanics & forecasting
  • Native email & calendar logging
  • Multi-user permissions & large-team workflows
  • Out-of-the-box dashboards, mobile apps, vendor support

We deliberately don't compete on funnel mechanics for a big team. This is built for a focused BD workflow — and if we ever need pipeline mechanics, this loop can feed a CRM rather than be replaced by one.

The cost a CRM hides — time Research & enrich, per contact: ~20–30 min by hand → 3–5 sec automated. Retrieval that takes minutes of digging in a CRM is one search here.
10–30× more throughput — one run handles 5 contacts or 500 at the same time cost, so volume never means another hire
Bottom line

A CRM would give us a differently-organised place to store contacts we'd still have to find ourselves. This gives us the contacts, the context, and a knowledge base that compounds — inside the tools the team already uses. It isn't a cheaper CRM. It's a different, and for our purpose better, machine.

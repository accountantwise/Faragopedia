A CRM remembers what you typed. Faragopedia understands what you know.

A CRM is a system of record — structured slots you fill in and later struggle to read back. Faragopedia is a system of understanding: it reads your material, writes it up as interlinked pages, and keeps the whole thing current on its own. The question was never “which is the better CRM?” It's that no CRM does this at all — and this is the capability the business actually lacked.

The core difference

Two ways to hold what you know

Point a CRM and Faragopedia at the same person and you get two fundamentally different objects. A CRM gives you a record — fields in a box, walled off from every other record. Faragopedia gives you a node in a living graph, wired to the company, the project, the people and the very sources each fact came from.

What it does differently

Three things a subscription CRM structurally will not do

01

It reads and writes itself

Drop in a document, an email, a web article or a PDF and the AI pulls out what matters, updates the right pages, and wires up the cross-references. A CRM's fields stay empty until a human types — and its free-text notes are where knowledge goes to die.

Ingestion: source in → linked pages out
02

Knowledge is linked, not filed

Every person, company and project is a node in a graph you can browse and see whole (the Link View). Relationships surface at a glance instead of hiding across unrelated records. A CRM can filter a list; it can't show you the shape of what you know.

View: the whole network, not one record
03

It keeps itself honest

A built-in health check finds orphaned pages, broken links, stale claims and contradictions — and proposes the fixes. CRM data quietly rots because nobody audits it; Faragopedia audits and maintains itself.

Upkeep: AI-audited, not left to rot

On cost & on fairness

The honest accounting

What a CRM leaves you paying for anyway

  • Per-seat CRM fees — $12–$175+ per user / month — plus onboarding of $1,500–$3,500
  • A separate wiki / knowledge base (Notion, Confluence) — a CRM can't hold synthesised knowledge
  • An AI or search add-on to make any of it retrievable
  • The human hours to keep notes written, linked and current

Typical 2026 market pricing. And it's rented: even fully paid-up, a CRM still can't read a document, link it, or keep it current — and the knowledge leaves when the subscription lapses.

Where a CRM genuinely wins

  • Deal pipeline, quotes & forecasting — Faragopedia has no sales funnel
  • Native email & calendar logging
  • Multi-user accounts & permissions — Faragopedia's MVP is single-tenant today (on the hardening roadmap)
  • Structured dashboards, mobile apps, vendor support

Faragopedia isn't a CRM replacement — it's the institutional-memory layer a CRM has never provided. Run a CRM for the pipeline if we need one; let Faragopedia hold the understanding.

Bottom line

A CRM would give us tidy records we'd still have to read, maintain and connect ourselves. Faragopedia reads our material, links it, keeps it current, and shows us the whole picture — self-hosted and ours. A CRM remembers what we typed. This understands what we know.
